Research

From field systems to benchmark validity

My work asks how real-world telemetry should be collected, organized, labeled, and evaluated before model scores can be treated as meaningful evidence.

Research statement

I am interested in trustworthy and data-centric machine learning for real-world IoT systems. My current research examines weakly labeled telemetry, where labels are constructed from rules, operational context, and incomplete observations rather than independent ground truth. I focus on the conditions under which a benchmark remains interpretable: traceable data construction, temporal separation, leakage control, matched comparisons, and transport across deployments. The long-term direction is to develop reliable evaluation and data systems for machine learning under imperfect sensing, missing context, and distribution shift.

Stage 01

Building an IoT telemetry system for durian orchards

Undergraduate thesis · 8.9/10
2026Advisor: Tran Hoai HanhVLUTE

The thesis developed an end-to-end system for collecting environmental and soil telemetry in a real cultivation environment. The system used ESP32-S3, SHT30, an ES-SOIL-7-IN-1 sensor, a SIM A7682S cellular module, and Firebase Realtime Database.

The project moved beyond displaying sensor values. Each record was designed as traceable telemetry containing measurements, timestamps, read status, transmission status, retry information, and operational metadata. A downstream pipeline transformed raw Firebase data into structured benchmark views with weak labels, temporal features, and time-aware evaluation.

  • Designed and deployed the sensing, transmission, storage, and web-monitoring pipeline.
  • Organized raw data through Layer0 and canonicalized sensor snapshots through Layer1.
  • Constructed weak operational labels and multiple benchmark representations.
  • Used temporal splitting and purge gaps to reduce optimistic evaluation.
  • Restricted interpretation to telemetry behavior rather than unsupported agronomic diagnosis.

Stage 02

Benchmarking tabular models on weakly supervised agricultural IoT data

Submitted to EAI AICON 2026
2026Co-authored manuscriptAgricultural IoT analytics

This work benchmarked XGBoost, FT-Transformer, TabM, and TabPFN on agricultural IoT data labeled through percentile-based weak supervision. It compared random and temporal split protocols and examined how model rankings changed under future-oriented evaluation.

My contribution focused on data cleaning, dataset organization, weak-label construction, experimental preparation, model evaluation, and analysis of the results. The work provided the immediate foundation for my later questions about whether strong performance reflects transferable knowledge or recovery of the labeling mechanism.

Stage 03

Observation contracts and effective evidence in temporal IoT targets

Internal review
First-author manuscriptUnder internal review prior to submission2026

This project investigates observation contracts and effective evidence in temporal rule-derived agricultural IoT targets. Weak labels are neither automatically correct nor automatically useless; their value depends on what information a model can recover, how the label is constructed over time, and whether learned relationships survive a change in deployment.

  • Representation control: compare feature views using matched anchors so that score changes are not confused with sample changes.
  • Temporal validity: separate point evidence from 3-hour and 8-hour history while controlling eligibility and leakage.
  • Rule recoverability: test whether models reproduce the labeling mechanism instead of learning independent structure.
  • Deployment transport: freeze thresholds, preprocessing, and models before evaluation on a changed field position.
  • Provenance: preserve the source of thresholds, assignments, evidence, and evaluation projections.

The intended contribution is an evaluation framework for identifying when benchmark scores become misleading under weak labels, temporal dependence, and deployment shift.
